# The travelers

There was this backpack. It was technically my dad's; I just kept borrowing it. I took it to England while I was 14, to western China later in high school, and to northern India during college. I don't even remember the brand; to me, it's just *the travel bag*.

My father swore by its versatility. It had a rollable cover to hide the shoulder straps, so it doubled as a duffel bag. When I rolled the cover up to my neck, I liked to imagine that I was carrying a sleeping bag, though I never camped in the wild.

![](/vault-assets/Pasted%20image%2020261023194728.png)

It finally became mine in 2025 (no, my dad's doing fine -- Thanks for asking). It survived another five trips between SFO and YYZ before I replaced it with a Patagonia on July 1, 2026.

The new backpack was a [Black Hole Micro MLC](https://www.patagonia.com/product/black-hole-micro-mlc-carry-on-backpack-22-liters/49260.html), Patagonia's attempt to squeeze as much travel utility as possible into a carry-on-sized bag. It had a few extra features, including a dedicated cross-body shoulder strap. All three shoulder straps can be tucked away in the back cushion, which looks less cool but much tidier than a roll-'em-up. That said, it was definitely an upgrade overall.

Why July? Mostly for my sabbatical. For a month, I traveled from California to France, China, Toronto, and then back. The bag held up well.

![](/vault-assets/Pasted%20image%2020261023195507.png)
*Le Patagonia, waiting in a hotel locker for my mom to pick up for laundry. I never grow up, do I.*

What made me truly appreciate it was my unexpected half-day layover in Shenzhen. I landed to find a notice that my connecting flight had been canceled, and sought help at the service desk. While the airline was preparing my accommodation, I quickly washed up in the restroom, changed, and even put on some hair gel. How glad I was to know that I didn't smell, when I realized that I was to share a ride with a gal. (Yes, I tried to rhyme.)

It was this college-aged Parisian's first solo trip abroad, and she was eager to explore. And so, a spontaneous little adventure was soon afoot. Before 9a.m., we'd already hiked to the top of [a mountain](https://maps.app.goo.gl/jC88TSJtjJycNWHx8), where a Buddhist temple stood. It was so modern that even the hermit's cave was air-conditioned. We watched janitors sweeping coins from a wishing well, as the summer drizzle cleared up.

![](/vault-assets/Pasted%20image%2020261023195200.png)
*There were little turtles crawling around. They were adorable.*

By the time we descended, we were hungry. As one should in Guangdong, we had dim sum for brunch. After returning to the hotel (courtesy of Hainan Airlines), we each had our second shower of the day. Let's just say that, with summer sweat, rain drops, and Worcestershire sauce on my shirt, I thanked myself -- and the spacious backpack -- for packing a change.

# The commuters

But once I returned to work, the backpack felt like overkill. I keep a work laptop at home and another in the office, so my daily carry is mostly an iPad mini, a gaming handheld, and a pair of VR glasses. In a 22L bag, they rattle around like loose change in a suitcase. Oof.

Even with these gadgets fastened up, I don't want to wear out a $150 backpack. So I went for something smaller and cheaper. Over the Amazon sale a few weeks back, I got a [Fjällräven Kanken](https://www.fjallraven.com/us/en-us/bags-gear/kanken/kanken-bags/kanken/) for half off (though the separately sold [shoulder pads](https://www.fjallraven.com/us/en-us/bags-gear/kanken/kanken-accessories/kanken-shoulder-pads/) ate into those savings). Thanks to the limited selection of colorways on sale, I picked a color that I had always been too (young and) self-conscious to wear, "Peach Sand-Terracotta Brown".

![](/vault-assets/Pasted%20image%2020261023201442.png)

This is actually my first Kanken. I've been a Herschel guy since college. Over the past decade, I've gone through two [Pop Quizzes](https://herschel.com/products/pop-quiz-backpack) and two [City Backpacks](https://herschel.com/products/city-backpack). One of the *Pop Quizzes* was featured in my 2024 post "[What's in my bag](https://blog.myli.page/whats-in-my-bag-eeef1a6c3434)", while one *City Backpack* earned a less honorable mention in [another](https://blog.myli.page/from-14-years-old-till-29-my-experience-with-desktop-linux-b798d07bcd00):

> On a particularly unlucky day in 2016, the cap on a bottle of *Simply Orange* went loose in my backpack. The bag was so watertight that 200ml of juice soaked through my MacBook Air for a good afternoon.

Truth be told, that's **not the first Apple device that I damaged in this backpack**. I did all my schoolwork on my iPad mini (1st gen) since 2014, so when the original iPad Pro came out, I was among its earliest buyers. I received the iPad before the case could arrive, so I was driving it as-is. When I hit the gym the next day, I had totally forgotten that I'd been carrying it around in this cushion-less backpack. As I always did, I threw the backpack into the locker. As the bag hit the floor, an instant regret struck me. *Crack* -- the tablet was ruined. What a lesson learned! To this day, I handle bags with extra care.

![](/vault-assets/Pasted%20image%2020261023203844.png)
*The unlucky canvas bag had a deep lichen green / khaki olive flap.*
# The hand-me-downs

Fast forward some three years. This *City Backpack* had earned a scar itself on the side, and I had been driving a *Pop Quiz*. I was then leaving grad school and moving out of my apartment in West Philly. A third-year student I worked with on research, who was moving off campus, came to scavenge my Ikea bed frame. Underprepared, I gave her this backpack to hold onto the bolts and the screws, thinking that would be the last time this old piece could be of some use.

Later that year, when she dropped by my workplace in San Bruno, she was carrying the backpack, its cut neatly patched with decorative tape. I hadn't expected to see it again, let alone repaired. We stayed in touch, and last month we even had lunch in Paris.

![](/vault-assets/Pasted%20image%2020261023210419.png)
*This was the Pop Quiz I used till 2019.*

That was not the first hand-me-down. I once had this *City Backpack* in light blue. Its faux leather strap broke in just 7 months (Apr 30, 2021 to Jan 8, 2022). With its warranty, I upgraded to a *Pop Quiz* for free and stowed away the *City Backpack*. The next use I found for it was as a laptop sleeve in July 2023, when I embarked on a 6-week vacation back to my home country. (Talking about all the PTOs one could save up during COVID.)

"It's perfectly usable," said my parents. Puzzled, I was taken to a shoe repair shop. In the hands of a skilled craftsperson, the strap was sewn back into place in under a minute. I could've had that done in San Jose, I realized. That was the moment when I noticed how unfamiliar I had become with handiwork.

Whatever happened to the kid who carved miniature skylines out of cardboard? The kid who made cameras out of toilet-paper rolls and magnifying glasses? The kid who strung a doorbell across the hallway with knitting wool?

I blame that on this desk job.

Normally, this is the point where I'd spell out a moral from the story. "Curb your consumerism," "one's trash is someone else's treasure," and the like. But I'll leave that to the reader. These are **just a few backpacks** about which I *happen to* have anecdotes to tell. They carried me through memorable adventures, expensive mistakes, long-lasting friendships, and the occasional reminder that some things are easier to fix than I'd thought.

They served me well.
